# 321 Genesis — English Translation Style Guide & Glossary

How the English edition is written. Every lesson is translated against this file.

> **Terminology authority: `en/GLOSSARY.md`.** That file reproduces the official 321
> Chinese–English term table, taken from the English edition of
> *321: Towards the End Time Revival* (V2) — the ministry's own published renderings, not
> translations invented here. Section 3 below is a working extract of it. **Where the two
> disagree, GLOSSARY.md wins**, and this file must be corrected to match.

## 1. Register

These are **spoken teaching manuscripts**, meant to be read aloud to a congregation or a small
group. The English must sound like a warm, literate preacher talking — not like a translated
document and not like an academic paper.

- Contractions are welcome where the Chinese is conversational: *you're, don't, it isn't*.
- Short sentences. The Chinese preaches in short bursts; keep that pulse.
- Rhetorical questions stay rhetorical questions.
- Direct address stays direct: 你 = **you** (singular, personal), 我們 = **we**.
- 弟兄姊妹 = **Brothers and sisters** (never "brethren", never dropped — it is the pulse of
  the manuscript and marks where the preacher turns to face the room).
- The em-dash rhythm of the Chinese (——) is part of the voice. Keep it as a spaced em-dash — like
  this — or recast the sentence; never collapse it into a comma splice.
- Never flatten an image into an abstraction. 「他把聲音交給了哭」 is *"he gave his voice over to
  weeping"*, not *"he wept loudly"*.
- No padding, no summarising, no smoothing away a hard sentence. Nothing is shortened.

## 4. Scripture

- Quotations use the **World English Bible (WEB)** — public domain, free to distribute.
- Quote it **verbatim**. Never paraphrase a verse the Chinese quotes verbatim, and never quote a
  verse the Chinese only alludes to.
- The Chinese quotes 和合本 inside 「」. In English: double quotation marks, with the reference
  in the same position the Chinese puts it: *"…" (Genesis 32:9–12)* or *Genesis 32:9 says, "…"*.
- Where 和合本 and WEB differ in a way the argument depends on (e.g. 和合本「空虛混沌」/ WEB
  "formless and empty"), quote WEB and let the Hebrew carry the point — the exegesis is always
  built on the Hebrew, never on the Chinese rendering.
- Chinese verse-number style 「三十二章九節」 becomes **32:9**; 「第九到十二節」 becomes
  **verses 9–12**. Book names in full on first mention in a paragraph, then bare references.

## 5. Original languages

Keep the script and the romanisation exactly as the Chinese has them:

> 希伯來文的「空虛混沌」是 תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ（tohu vavohu）
> The Hebrew behind "formless and empty" is תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ (*tohu vavohu*).

- Hebrew and Greek keep their vowel points / breathings — never strip them.
- Romanisation goes in *italics* in English (the Chinese has no italics; this is an English
  convention worth adding).
- Grammatical labels: Piel → Piel, Hiphil → Hiphil, Hithpael → Hithpael, 不定詞加強句法 →
  the infinitive absolute, 反身式 → the reflexive, 使役語幹 → the causative stem,
  雙數形 → the dual, 疊韻詞 → a rhyming pair, 字根 → the root, 字面是 → literally.

## 7. Things that must not drift

1. **Nothing is shortened.** A 5,000-character Chinese lesson becomes a full English lesson of
   the same substance. If an English paragraph is markedly shorter than its Chinese block,
   something was dropped.
2. **Pastoral safety language is load-bearing.** Every leader's reminder about abuse, grief,
   infertility, self-harm or professional help is translated in full and never softened.
   「必要時協助尋求專業協助」 = *help them find professional support where that is needed* —
   keep it.
3. **The exegesis is not negotiable.** Word counts ("this word appears ten times in chapter 1"),
   verse numbers, grammatical claims and cross-references are checked against the text, not
   translated loosely.
4. **The 321 terms above are fixed.** A synonym is a bug. They are the ministry's published
   English (`en/GLOSSARY.md`); improving on them is not the translator's job.
